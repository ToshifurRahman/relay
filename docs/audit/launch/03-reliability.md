Live probes, verbatim (read-only, low volume):

```
GET /health/live   -> 200 {"status":"ok"}   22.5s (cold) / 0.13s / 0.11s / 0.09s (warm)
GET /health/ready  -> 200 {"status":"ok"}   0.10s / 0.11s / 0.10s (warm)
```

The first `/health/live` after the service had spun down took **22.5s**; every later call was warm
(0.09s to 0.13s). That cold figure is the number that matters for REL-06: a 30s client timeout leaves
about 7.5s of margin over the observed cold start, which is thin, so the timeout is reported as a 408
that `query-client.ts` retries rather than as a dead end. A cold start slower than 30s therefore costs
one retry, not a stuck page. The cold-start behavior itself is documented in `docs/deployment.md`
"Free plan behavior".

## Settings left unchanged

Two settings were reviewed and deliberately left as they are, each an accepted risk recorded
elsewhere:

- `render.yaml` keeps `healthCheckPath: /health/ready`. Pointing it at `/health/live` would stop the
  health check from catching a database that is down, which is the failure it exists to catch. The
  cost of the Postgres query on every check is recorded as `COST-03`/`COST-04` in
  `docs/audit/launch/04-cost-limits.md`, and the free-plan consequence is spelled out in
  `docs/deployment.md` "Free plan behavior".
- `SNS_ALERT_MAX_PER_HOUR` stays at `20`. The cap is a flood guard, not a hard limit, and the free
  SNS allowance is 1,000 emails a month; see REL-07 above.
